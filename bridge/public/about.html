This is a rough research prototype. The kids in it are made up. It has never been used with a real person. It is not a product, not a medical device, and not clinical advice.

What this is

Bridge helps the adult standing next to a kid who has trouble communicating.

Some kids say things in ways most people can't read: a word repeated twice, pacing by a door, a line from a TV show, a "yes" that really means "hello." The people who know that kid well can read it. A new counselor, a substitute teacher, or a bus driver can't.

Bridge writes down what the people who know the kid have learned, and helps everyone else use it.

Where the idea came from

It started at summer camp. The author was a counselor for kids with developmental disabilities from 1989 to 1998. What helped a kid most wasn't the picture schedule on the wall. It was a person who knew that kid's "language" standing next to it, every time. That person can't be everywhere. Bridge is an attempt to help with that.

How it works

1. A profile for each kid. Think of it as orientation day written down: what their words, sounds and gestures mean, what upsets them and the early warning signs, what calms them, their daily schedule, and who they trust.

2. Tools that read the profile. Each tab in the demo is one tool:

What did that mean?Describe what the kid did. Get a few possible meanings, each with how sure it is. If you figure out the real meaning, you can save your own interpretation.
Help them say itThe kid picks words. Bridge suggests short messages that use only those words. The kid picks one, or none.
Plan changedLunch is late, swim is cancelled. Get the exact words and cues to explain it to this kid.
How do I talk with them?A one-card briefing for someone meeting the kid for the first time.
Story for an eventA short story that prepares the kid for something coming up, like a fire drill or a dentist visit.
Log a moment / PatternsRecord what happened. Over time, look for patterns, with a way to test each one.
Review queueAnything Bridge thinks it learned waits here. Nothing goes into a kid's profile until a person approves it.
ProfileEverything Bridge knows about this kid, and where each piece came from.

The rules it follows

  • The AI suggests. People decide. Every answer is a guess for a person to check.
  • It never speaks for the kid. It only drafts from words the kid chose, and the code checks that no words were added or dropped.
  • It can't change a profile on its own. A person has to approve every change.
  • Your guess is labeled as a guess. When a counselor saves what they think something meant, it's marked as their interpretation, not a fact.
  • The schedule is never AI. For a kid who needs to know what's next, the timing has to be exactly right every time, so it's plain code.
  • It knows when it doesn't fit. If the people who know a kid haven't allowed a tool, that tool won't run for that kid.

The kids in the demo

All seven are made up. Each is loosely inspired by camp memories, with names and details changed. Each one tests a different part of the idea.

  • Felix needs to know what's next. A late lunch can undo him.
  • Gus talks in route numbers and highway exits.
  • Ivy talks through songs, but only with people she has chosen.
  • Miles doesn't feel pain, and loves pranks.
  • Rowan doesn't speak, and nobody knows yet how much he understands.
  • Theo hurts himself when he sees a dog or a balloon.
  • Walt says "yes" to everything, so "yes" doesn't mean yes.

Try this

  1. Pick Felix. Open Plan changed, say lunch is 20 minutes late, and see how it explains the change to him.
  2. Pick Ivy. Open How do I talk with them? See why a stranger shouldn't sing to her.
  3. Pick Walt. In What did that mean?, describe him saying "yes." See that it won't treat that as agreement.
  4. Pick Rowan. Try Help them say it. It refuses, because drafting words for a kid who can't yet choose would put words in his mouth.

About this demo

  • Answers come from a free AI model (gpt-oss-120b on Groq). The label at the top right shows which one is answering.
  • To keep it free, the demo gives a limited number of AI answers per day. After that, answers come from a simple rule-based version, and the page tells you when that happens.
  • Everyone using this page shares the same demo data. It resets from time to time.
  • Profile editing is turned off here. You can edit profiles if you run it on your own computer.

What's missing

The most important thing: real profiles, written by people who actually work with these kids. The goal is to get feedback from speech therapists, teachers, aides and parents on what a profile needs to say.
